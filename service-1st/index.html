What We Heard

700-800 quotes a month. Nobody following up on all of them.

Service 1st is generating a serious volume of bids across three divisions: plumbing, HVAC, and electrical. The diagnosis and estimating work gets done. The quote goes out. And then a large chunk of them just sit there because the estimators have other things to handle.

The manual tracking spreadsheet that Chris built is a real attempt to solve this, but it doesn't scale across five estimators and three divisions. Here's what that problem looks like in real numbers:

  • Roughly 30% of bids go unresolved each month because nobody followed up. That's 210-240 quotes per month going dark.
  • Customers are telling you they never even received the quote. The issue isn't just follow-up. The first confirmation isn't happening either.
  • Each bid costs $200-$300 to generate between the diagnostic visit and estimator time. When it goes unworked, that's a sunk cost with zero return.
  • Build Ops tracks work orders, not sales. There's no pipeline view, no follow-up task system, and no way to see where a quote stands across all three divisions at once.
  • The spreadsheet only works when estimators use it consistently, and right now only a few of the five estimators are using it reliably.

How We're Building It

Three tools. One automated system. No more spreadsheet.

The build connects the tools you already use to a sales and marketing layer they're currently missing. Here's how the three pieces fit together:

Build Ops
Field Service Platform
→
Make.com
Automation Bridge
→
HighLevel
Sales CRM + Automation

Build Ops stays exactly as you use it today. When a quote is marked "Sent," Make.com automatically picks it up and pushes the contact and job information into HighLevel. From there, HighLevel fires a three-touch follow-up sequence. The spreadsheet goes away and the process runs on its own.

1

Day of Quote

An email and text go out confirming the customer received the quote and letting them know who to contact with questions. Comes from the estimator who wrote it, not a generic company address.

2

Day 2 Follow-Up

A second email and text checks in, answers any common questions, and nudges the customer toward a decision. Still from the estimator. Personal, not automated-feeling.

3

Day 4 Follow-Up

The final touchpoint. After this, the sequence stops. No annoying over-contact. If the job gets approved at any point, the sequence stops automatically. Same for rejections.

Portal contacts (ServiceChannel and ResQ, restaurant work) are automatically excluded from the sequence. Apartment complex and direct commercial contacts move through it as normal.

Team Onboarding
