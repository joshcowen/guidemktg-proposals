Outbound Client Acquisition Build

Phase one of the outbound program: what we're building, how it works, what it costs, and when it goes live.

What We Heard

A small, finite market, and a plan to reach every company in it.

The client is a managed IT services provider operating in three North Carolina metros. They only sell to companies of 15 to 125 employees that run fully on Microsoft 365 and are willing to hand their whole technology function to one provider. That works out to roughly 2,500 to 4,000 companies. The goal is to find all of them, confirm they fit before anyone makes contact, and reach each one before their current IT arrangement comes up for review.

  • Fit has to be verified before contact. A company on Google Workspace can't become a client, so no sales time should go to it.
  • Every first email needs something true and specific to lead with, or it gets deleted like the rest. The plan is a real finding about each company's own email security, with a one-page report they can check themselves.
  • Almost no MSP tracks when each prospect's contract can be exited. The program is built around capturing that renewal date on every account, so month thirteen produces more than month one.
  • Some events reliably send a company shopping: their IT provider gets acquired, their IT person quits, a new CFO or COO starts, they move offices, or they grow past 15 people. Those accounts need to jump the queue.
  • Outreach can't put the client's day-to-day email at risk, so sending has to happen from separate, warmed-up domains.
  • The client provides a dedicated caller and Spartan handles the messaging. What's missing is the engine underneath: the list, the verification, the infrastructure, and the tracking.

1

Build the account list

Every company with 15 to 125 employees across the three metros, somewhere between 4,000 and 6,000 businesses, named and mapped by territory.

2

Check Microsoft 365

A company's email settings are public. We read them for every company on the list. Google companies come off, so no sales time goes to them.

3

Find the security problem

The same lookup shows whether a company has protected its domain against forged email. We record exactly what's wrong and generate a one-page branded report for each company.

4

Find the decision-makers

Two to three contacts per company across owner, finance, and operations. Companies this size don't have a CIO, so the IT vendor call usually sits with the CFO, controller, or operations lead.

5

Set up safe sending

Twelve inboxes on lookalike domains, authenticated and warmed for four weeks. Outreach never touches the client's own site or email.

6

Track renewals and triggers

Renewal date becomes a required field on every account. A weekly alert flags companies that just became worth calling, and a dashboard shows sends, replies, dates captured, and meetings set.
